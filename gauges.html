Gauges — The Instrument Panel

Auto-refreshes every 60s while open. Position for THETA WAR is read from what you saved in COACH.
Trend Force—
The math: every signal (VWAP stretch, 9EMA slope, MACD, RSI, multi-timeframe) is converted to a z-score — how many standard deviations from its own recent normal — so the gauge self-calibrates to each ticker's personality. The weighted sum passes through tanh (a saturation curve: no single signal can peg the needle) and is then multiplied by an ADX damper — in chop, the whole needle deflates toward zero, because trend readings inside chop are statistically meaningless. Right of center = bull force, left = bear force. Beyond ±60 = strong; a needle pinned past ±85 is stretched — late to chase.
Fuel—
The math: participation measured three ways — RVOL (today's cumulative volume ÷ the average at this exact time of day over prior sessions — the honest comparison), range expansion (current ATR vs its session baseline), and squeeze state (Bollinger inside Keltner = compressed energy). Trend Force says which way the car points; Fuel says whether there's gas. A big Trend reading on an empty Fuel tank is the classic trap — a drift, not a move. Below 35 = no trade regardless of direction.
Theta War — your position—
The math: from the live chain, your contract's delta and theta are computed via Black-Scholes (IV back-solved from the actual mid). Then the war, in dollars per hour per contract: what delta earns at the tape's current pace — Δ × (ATR-per-hour) — versus what theta charges to sit — |Θ| ÷ 6.5 trading hours. Needle green: movement is out-earning decay; the option pays rent. Needle red: you are paying by the hour to hold a hope. This gauge is why scalps have time stops — when the tape slows, the war flips even while price goes nowhere.
