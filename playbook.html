4a · MASTERCLASS — The 9 vs The 20

What they are. The 20 SMA is democracy — every one of the last 20 closes gets an equal vote, even the one from 100 minutes ago. The 9 EMA is a weighted vote — recent closes count far more, so it turns the moment price turns. You are not watching two lines; you are watching an argument between the present (the 9) and the recent past (the 20).

The four states — and only one is fully tradeable:

1 · RIDE9 above 20, both rising, price riding the 9. The ONLY state where the scalp book is fully open. Enter on the pullback that touches the 9 and holds — the trend inhaling.
2 · STRETCHEDPrice far above the 9 (3–4 ATRs). A chase, not an entry — the 9 is an anchor price returns to. Late buyers here are the snapback's fuel.
3 · BRAID9 and 20 flat and twisted, price whipping through both. Chop — ADX under 20 — the account killer. Every crossover inside a braid is a lie. NO TRADE, regardless of other signals.
4 · CROSS9 crosses under 20 after a trend: the present out-voted the past. Late as an exit (the 9-EMA close already got you out) but a hard NO-NEW-LONGS gate, and the first structural sign of a short.

Why the 20 is "the first wall." Institutions use the 20-period area as a value reference, so resting bids sit there. First touch usually rejects — a discounted entry with a tight stop below. But each test eats the resting orders: the second or third touch in the same move usually breaks. First touch = buy the dip. Third touch = the floor is hollow, tighten up.

The gap is a health meter. Strong trends EXPAND the 9-to-20 gap. If price makes a new high but the gap is narrower than at the last high, the move is dying while the chart still looks fine — the structural version of a fading MACD. Watch for it 10:30–11:00 CT, where morning trends go to fade.

One sentence: the 20 decides WHETHER there's a trend, the 9 decides WHERE you enter and WHEN you leave, and the space between them says how much life is left. Before every trigger: 60 seconds narrating those two lines — which state, which touch, expanding or narrowing. That's the whole read.
