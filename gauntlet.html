ATR — the stock's normal step size

ATR (Average True Range) measures how far a stock typically moves in one candle — its normal breathing, in dollars. On the 5-minute chart, the 5-minute ATR is the average full height (high to low, including any gap) of the last 14 five-minute candles. It is the single most important number for sizing a trade, because a dollar means something different on a $40 stock than on a $940 one — but one ATR always means the same thing: one normal move.

Why the Gauntlet is built on it. Every level you see was sized in ATRs so it fits the stock you're actually trading:

STOP = the 9EMA/VWAP shelf, minus a 0.15-ATR buffer. A stop closer than the noise gets hit by ordinary breathing; the ATR buffer keeps it just beyond.

T1 (first target) = trigger ± 2.2 ATR. About two normal moves — ambitious but reachable on a real push, not a fantasy.

TRAILING STOP (after T1) = 1.5 × ATR × delta. The leash must be longer than one breath or the wiggle stops you out.

SCORE includes an "it can travel" test (ATR ÷ price ≥ 0.18%) — the machine refuses to rank a stock that doesn't move enough to pay.

A full worked example — XOM, 5-minute ATR = $0.28:

That $0.28 means XOM has moved about 28¢ per 5-minute candle lately.

• A stop only 15¢ away sits inside the noise — you'd be stopped by random ticks. Bad.
• The Gauntlet's stop sits ~0.7 ATR below the shelf (~$0.42 of room) — beyond the breathing, so only a real reversal triggers it.
• T1 at 2.2 ATR = 2.2 × 0.28 ≈ $0.62 of stock travel from the trigger — reachable on one good push.
• The trailing stop = 1.5 × 0.28 × 0.70Δ ≈ $0.30 on the option — rides just outside XOM's normal wiggle.

Now compare SPCX with a $0.70 ATR: the very same formulas give a wider stop, a ~$1.54 T1 distance, and a $0.75 trail — 2½× everything — because SPCX breathes 2½× harder. Same rules, sized to the animal. That is why one fixed dollar stop can't work across stocks, and why the Gauntlet does the ATR math for you on every name.

Rule of thumb: stops beyond one ATR, targets a few ATRs out, trails about 1.5 ATR behind. Watch it change through the day — rising ATR means the stock is getting wild; size down.

THE RUN METER — where is the stock in its run?

The problem it solves: being right on the stock and wrong on the clock. A stock can be strong (high score) and its move already spent (late). Entering a spent move is the trade that reverses the moment you're in — you bought the top of the cycle. The Run Meter answers one question, 0–100: how much of this run has already happened?

DAY RANGE USED (40%) — today's high-to-low versus a normal day (the average of recent sessions). At 35% the day has room; at 80% it's nearing the end; past 100% the day has already delivered more than usual and every further push is an outlier. A breakout after 90% of the range is spent needs the day to do something abnormal to pay you.

STRETCH FROM VWAP (30%) — how many standard deviations price sits from the day's volume-weighted fair value, in your direction. ±1σ is normal. ±2σ is extended — where chasers get hurt. ±3σ is exhaustion — a fade zone, not an entry.

LEG TRAVEL (20%) — how far price has moved since the last 9EMA/20SMA cross, in ATRs. Under 2 = a young leg. Over 5 = an old one that has already paid the early buyers.

RSI (10%) — over 70 (or under 30 for puts) = stretched. Context: on a trend day it's a mile marker, not a stop sign — which is why it's weighted lightly.

The phases and what to do:

EARLY (0–29) — coiling near fair value, little range used. Don't front-run. Wait for the trigger.
FRESH (30–49) — the sweet spot. A fired trigger here has most of the day ahead of it. Enter on the trigger.
RUNNING (50–69) — mid-life. Only a PULLBACK entry: price returns to the 9 EMA / VWAP, holds, pushes off on volume. Never the breakout candle. Half size, closer target.
LATE (70–84) — you'd be buying the top of the cycle. No chase. Wait for the reversion or tomorrow. The Hunt will not sound the alarm here even if a trigger fires.
EXHAUSTED (85+) — reversal risk. Only the fade is a trade, and that isn't your playbook. Pass.

Worked example — SMCI, Friday 10:28 AM: up 6% on the day, range used 92% of a normal day, +2.4σ above VWAP, leg 5.8 ATR old, RSI 74 → RUN 86, EXHAUSTED. The score was 78 (strong stock) — and the Meter said the move was spent. That combination — high score, late run — is exactly the trade that reverses on entry. The right play: wait for a pullback to VWAP that holds, and enter the next leg fresh.

The Meter also feeds selection: LATE costs a stock 15 score points, EXHAUSTED costs 30 — so a spent move can't top your board just because the stock is strong. Market forces and the choice are now the same calculation.
